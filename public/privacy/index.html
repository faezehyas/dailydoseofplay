Privacy

No ads, no analytics, and accounts are optional: every game works without one. Here is everything the site keeps, and who sees what while you play.

On your device

Your browser keeps your choices for this site in its local storage. Your nickname goes to the people you play with, and a host's game settings go to their guests; nothing else leaves your device.

  • ddp-name: your nickname.
  • ddp-settings: your general settings, once you change one: the theme, whether sounds are on and how loud, whether leaving a game with friends asks first, and whether other players' reactions are hidden.
  • ddp-theme and ddp-sound: where earlier visits kept the theme and whether sounds were on. They move into ddp-settings on your next visit.
  • Each game's settings and which of them you picked yourself, including the robot's level and, in games for more than two, how many players a robot game has:
    • Tic Tac Toe: ddp-ttt-settings
    • Connect 4: ddp-c4-settings
    • Gomoku: ddp-gomoku-settings
    • Chess: ddp-chess-settings
    • Checkers: ddp-checkers-settings
    • Backgammon: ddp-bg-settings
    • Sea Battle: ddp-sb-settings
    • Chutes and Ladders: ddp-cl-settings
    • Dots and Boxes: ddp-db-settings
    • Ludo: ddp-ludo-settings
    • Crazy Eights: ddp-crazy-eights-settings
    • Go Fish: ddp-go-fish-settings
    • Gin Rummy: ddp-gin-rummy-settings
    • Hearts: ddp-hearts-settings
  • ddp-robot-record: your wins, draws and losses against the robots in each game, by how many players sat at the table, shown with Play vs robot, where Reset clears it.
  • ddp-lessons: which tutorial lessons you finished in each game, for the ticks in its lesson list.
  • ddp-last-game: the last game you opened. The site no longer saves it, but an earlier visit may have left it behind. Clearing your data removes it.

If you sign in

Signing in with Google is optional. It gives you a public name nobody else can take, which leaderboards will show. If you never sign in, the site sets no cookies and keeps nothing about you.

When you sign in, the site keeps, in its database:

  • your Google account ID, a number Google gives your account, so the next sign-in finds the same account;
  • the email address Google sends, shown only to you on your account page;
  • your public name, which other players see in games and on leaderboards, and when the account was made;
  • for each device you're signed in on, a session: a fingerprint of its cookie (not the cookie itself) and when it ends.

Your browser keeps one cookie, __Host-ddp_sid, that keeps you signed in. It lasts 30 days from your last visit and only ever goes to this site. While you sign in, two short-lived cookies hold the sign-in's details: __Host-ddp_oauth for 10 minutes, and __Host-ddp_signup for 30 minutes while a new account picks its name.

The site asks Google only for your account ID and email address, nothing else. Google knows you signed in to Daily Dose of Play. The database is hosted by Wasmer, which runs the site, in the same region as its server. IP addresses are never stored in it.

Your data stays until you delete the account. On your account page, Delete account removes your account ID, email address, public name and every session at once. Sign out everywhere ends every session but keeps the account.

The lobby server

Against the robot, the game runs in your browser alone. To play friends, your browser talks to the lobby server only while players connect. It sees your nickname, the game, the room code and your IP address, and it passes on the connection details your browsers swap to find each other. All of this lives in the server's memory, and the room is gone once the game starts, the host leaves or 30 minutes pass.

The server logs two lines per room, such as room ludo/ABCD created by 12 and room ludo/ABCD closed: the game, the room code and a connection number that counts up from 1 and isn't tied to you. Nicknames and IP addresses are never logged. To stop one device from flooding the server, it counts each IP address's open connections, open rooms and failed joins in memory; the failed joins are forgotten after a minute. Sign-in requests are counted the same way, per IP address in memory: for a minute, and for an hour for new accounts.

Playing over WebRTC

Moves go straight from browser to browser over WebRTC, not through the server. To connect, the browsers learn each other's IP address:

  • Two players: each browser sees the other's.
  • Three or four players: every guest connects to the host, so the host's browser sees every guest's address and each guest sees the host's.
  • Your browser asks public STUN servers run by Google and Cloudflare for its public address, so they see it too.

There's no relay server to pass moves along in between, so your address can't be hidden from the players you connect to. An IP address can show roughly where you are, so play with people you know.

Clearing your data

Open your browser's settings for this site and choose Clear site data (some browsers call it Delete data or Manage website data). That removes everything your device keeps, and signs you out. It doesn't delete an account: do that on your account page. A private window keeps nothing once you close it.
